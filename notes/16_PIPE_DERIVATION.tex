Laminar Pipe Derive

CYLINDRICAL PIPE: radial r, radius R, axial z.
Assume steady, laminar, Newtonian, fully developed axisymmetric flow: axial u(r), vr=vtheta=0.

HORIZONTAL PIPE (no axial gravity)
$$\frac{\mu}{r}\frac{d}{dr}(r\frac{du}{dr})=\frac{dp}{dz}$$
Do NOT use plane-flow u'' for a pipe.

STEP 1: multiply by r/mu:
$$\frac{d}{dr}(ru')=\frac{r}{\mu}\frac{dp}{dz}$$
STEP 2: integrate:
$$ru'=\frac{r^2}{2\mu}\frac{dp}{dz}+C_1$$
STEP 3: divide by r:
$$u'=\frac{r}{2\mu}\frac{dp}{dz}+\frac{C_1}{r}$$
STEP 4: integrate:
$$u=\frac{r^2}{4\mu}\frac{dp}{dz}$$
$$+C_1\ln r+C_2$$

FINITE CENTERLINE: C1=0 (log term would diverge at r=0).
NO SLIP: u(R)=0:
$$C_2=-\frac{R^2}{4\mu}\frac{dp}{dz}$$

FINAL PROFILE
$$u(r)=\frac{1}{4\mu}\frac{dp}{dz}(r^2-R^2)$$
Equivalently:
$$u(r)=-\frac{1}{4\mu}\frac{dp}{dz}(R^2-r^2)$$
Positive flow along +z requires dp/dz < 0 in horizontal pipe.

ANNULUS: no fluid at r=0; do NOT set log coefficient to 0 automatically. Use 2 wall BCs.
